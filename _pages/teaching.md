I have taught physics at George Washington University as a teaching assistant since 2021, in four courses. The one I have taught most is Nonlinear Dynamics, a final-year undergraduate course that broadly follows Strogatz's *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*. The other three are introductory calculus-based courses in mechanics and electromagnetism, including a two-semester sequence for life-science students, all taught in the SCALE-UP format: students spend much of each class working through problems in groups, and I guide the groups and lead the class discussions.
{: .page-intro}

Over these years I have taken on more of the teaching myself. In my first year I started running office hours as short tutorials, based on the Cambridge supervisions I was taught through: I go over the material students are unsure of, then set problems for them to work through and discuss. I also started going through each quiz in front of the class, so that common mistakes are dealt with before the course moves on. In 2022 I received the Bennhold Family Award for best graduate-student teaching, and in Nonlinear Dynamics I now also set quiz questions.

I am also prepared to teach mathematics courses, including calculus, linear algebra, ordinary differential equations and probability.
