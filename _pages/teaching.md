I have taught physics at George Washington University as a teaching assistant since 2021, and in 2022 I received the Bennhold Family Award for best graduate-student teaching. I began with introductory calculus-based courses in mechanics and electromagnetism, taught in the SCALE-UP format. The course I have taught most is PHYS 3127, a final-year undergraduate course on nonlinear dynamics that broadly follows Strogatz's *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos*. In all of them, students work through problems in groups during class, and I guide the groups and lead the discussions.
{: .page-intro}

Over these years I have taken on more of the teaching myself. In my first year I started running office hours as short tutorials, based on the Cambridge supervisions I was taught through: I go over the material students are unsure of, then set problems for them to work through and discuss. I also started going through each quiz in front of the class, so that common mistakes are dealt with before the course moves on. In PHYS 3127 I now run the quizzes from start to finish: I write the questions, grade the answers and review them with the class.
